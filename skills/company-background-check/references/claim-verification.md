# Targeted claim verification

Use this reference when the reader asks to verify a claim about funding, a named customer, a public contract or a regulated activity, or when one of these claims could materially change the employer assessment. Choose only the checks relevant to the company, the claim and the reader's decision. A routine absence of public detail is not a reason to investigate every category. Use public sources. Leave purchases and outreach to the reader.

## Set the target and keep a ledger

Resolve the employer's legal name, company number and jurisdiction first, using the website and register as in the skill. Identify the precise claim, date and entity making or receiving it. A brand, parent, subsidiary, contracting supplier and licensed entity can be different. Evidence each link to the employer and give its dates. Attribute rights, revenue and findings to the entity the source names.

Reuse the claim ledger in [Evidence, funding and ownership](evidence-and-funding.md). For each material claim add the relevant activity, location and period, claimed status, source's direct evidence, entity identifiers and the bridge to the employer. Record what the source actually supports, any contradiction and the next evidence that would resolve it. Capture exact URLs, publication and event dates and inspection limits. Treat copies of one announcement as one source.

Prefer an original, dated record from a customer, investor, filing body, contracting authority or relevant regulator, read at its actual scope. Company statements and credible reporting can establish that a claim was made, but label them as attributed. Search snippets and a matching web hit are leads, not proof. Open the record and match its identifiers and context before relying on it. A failed search or inaccessible record is an evidence limit, not proof the claim is false or misconduct occurred.

Stop when the material claim has suitably direct evidence and its date and scope are clear, or when proportionate public searches and accessible original records leave a named gap. Record that gap and the most useful next evidence. Do not turn a missing public record into a clean bill of health or a finding of wrongdoing.

## Funding

Identify whether the claim concerns equity, debt, grant or another instrument, a single round or a cumulative figure, and the amount and currency. Trace an announcement to the named investor's own statement or a relevant filing, including its terms and date where available. An investor's confirmation of a commitment corroborates that commitment. Look for direct evidence of closing, drawdown or payment before saying those occurred. Distinguish announced, committed, closed, drawn and paid statuses, and attribute the most advanced status that the evidence actually supports.

When a Companies House filing records an allotment, read the cash or non-cash consideration, paid and unpaid amounts and any correction or replacement. The nominal value alone establishes share capital. Relevant [Companies House event-driven filing guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/life-of-a-company-event-driven-filings/life-of-a-company-part-2-event-driven-filings) explains filing types. Reconcile overlapping rounds and conflicting accounts using the funding reference. Even a substantiated historic receipt does not prove current cash, burn or runway.

## Named customer and public contracts

For a material customer claim, seek a dated customer-authored case study, deployment announcement or other direct customer evidence identifying the product, legal or operational parties, activity and period. Distinguish a pilot, trial, planned implementation, live deployment and historic use. Describe customer-confirmed deployment at the stated time and scope. Require separate evidence for claims about ongoing use, payment, recurring revenue or contract size. Supplier marketing, a logo or a partnership announcement alone cannot establish a live deployment. Retain a precise attributed claim when stronger evidence is unavailable.

For a public-sector claim, identify the buyer, supplier, contract or framework reference, dates, subject and value basis, then inspect the linked notices and documents. Follow any supplier-to-employer bridge separately. Use the notice's own procurement regime and wording. Call-offs under legacy frameworks follow the applicable [transitional rules](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-plan-phase/guidance-transitional-and-saving-arrangements-html). Distinguish:

- **Framework admission:** a supplier is eligible for opportunities under a framework. This does not establish a call-off contract, delivery or revenue.
- **Actual contract:** the parties have entered into a specific contract or call-off. Under the Procurement Act 2023, a contract award notice records an intention to enter into a contract. A contract details notice records a contract the parties have entered into. Check whether it describes the framework itself or a specific call-off.
- **Delivery and money:** look for buyer acceptance or performance records tied to the contract and period. Attribute a recorded payment to the payer and recipient. Use a filing or other direct evidence identifying the contract before claiming recognised revenue. Label a contract estimate or ceiling as such.

Start with [Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Search), [Contracts Finder](https://www.gov.uk/contracts-finder) or the relevant authority's published records. The government guidance on [frameworks](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-define-phase/guidance-frameworks-html), [contract details notices](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-procure-phase/guidance-contract-details-notices-html) and [public tendering](https://www.gov.uk/tendering-for-public-sector-contracts) helps interpret the record. Check other jurisdiction-specific sources where appropriate.

## Regulated activity

Choose the official register or enforcement source for the actual activity and jurisdiction. Match legal name, company number or regulator identifier, location, permissions, status, effective dates and activity. Read conditions, limitations, cancellation and relevant enforcement records when material. Distinguish an investigation or proposed action from a final decision and record any published appeal or current outcome. Identify whether the entry belongs to the employer, another group entity or an individual and evidence the bridge. A registration in one entity or for one activity does not license the employer or every activity. A name-only result remains a lead. If the search gives no verified match, state which activity, identifiers, dates and sources you checked and leave permission unresolved.

Use sources such as these where applicable.

- For financial services, use the [FCA Financial Services Register](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/financial-services-register) and [FCA enforcement](https://www.fca.org.uk/about/how-we-regulate/enforcement). Distinguish authorisation, registration and appointed representative status. For a representative, check the appointment, principal and activities it covers.
- For care in England, use [CQC care services](https://www.cqc.org.uk/care-services). In Scotland, select the regulator for the service and use its public records, such as the [Care Inspectorate reports](https://www.mygov.scot/care-service-inspection-reports).
- For gambling in Great Britain, use the [Gambling Commission business register](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-register/businesses).
- For environmental permits in England, use the [Environment Agency public registers](https://environment.data.gov.uk/public-register/view/index).

Use the responsible body and register for other activities or jurisdictions. State what the official record establishes as at its date, and distinguish permission from evidence of compliance, enforcement action or actual operations. A search returning no relevant action does not prove none exists outside the checked source and period.

## Fit the existing report

Keep all eleven sections, the eight scorecard areas and existing flag severities and rollup. Put funding amounts, dates and status in section 4. Append optional plain-list lines for material customer, contract and regulatory checks under section 8, stating the evidence, entity, scope and date. Put independently substantiated adverse findings in section 1 under the existing rules, naming whose record they are. Do not make an unresolved claim a flag by itself.

Record material gaps, failed retrievals, unresolved entity bridges and conflicting claims in section 10. Apply the scorecard's Caution rule for a material gap in coverage and its usual rules for established findings. In section 9 rank the most decision-changing follow-ups, such as customer confirmation of live use, the entered call-off and delivery record, investor evidence of completed funding, or the correct licence and entity. Give precise source links in section 11 and wherever the report makes a claim.
